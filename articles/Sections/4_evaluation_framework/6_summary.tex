\subsection{Summary}

We present a multi-dimensional LLM-as-a-judge evaluation framework for ill-defined tasks, designed to move beyond scalar scoring and ground-truth-based evaluation.
The framework reflects key aspects of ill-defined problem solving, including problem-structuring, solution quality and reflective reasoning.

Empirical results demonstrate that the proposed evaluation procedure is both reliable and informative. High inter-rater reliability (ICC) indicates that aggregated LLM-based judgments are stable across repeated evaluation runs, while model comparison results show that the framework consistently discriminates between models of different capability levels.

These results suggest that the framework provides a reliable and informative measurement layer for ill-defined problem settings. In the following sections we build on this evaluation protocol to investigate and compare LLM-MAS approaches for solving ill-defined problems.
