\subsection{Validation via Model Comparison}

To assess the validity of the proposed evaluation framework, we examine whether it can discriminate between models of different capabilities. We evaluate three models: deepseek-v4-pro, gpt-4o-mini, and phi-4-mini-instruct.

For each problem-solution pair $(p_k,s_{k,q})$ we obtain an aggregated evaluation vector $\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(p_k, s_{k,q})}$ as defined in Section \ref{subsection:evaluation_procedure}.
To obtain model-level scores we aggregate these vectors across all problem-solution pairs associated with the given model $M_q$:

\[
\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(q)} = \frac{1}{|P_q|} \sum_{(p_k,s_{k,q}) \in P_q} \bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(p_k, s_{k,q})}
\]

where $P_q$ denotes the set of problem-solution pairs generated by model $M_q$. The resulting model-level scores are reported in Table \ref{table:model_comparison}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
\textbf{Criterion} & \textbf{deepseek-v4-pro} & \textbf{gpt-4o-mini} & \textbf{phi-4-mini} \\
\hline
Coherence & 4.23 $\pm$ 0.42 & 3.79 $\pm$ 0.41 & 3.39 $\pm$ 0.49 \\
Decomposition & 4.25 $\pm$ 0.43 & 3.57 $\pm$ 0.50 & 3.21 $\pm$ 0.41 \\
Diversity & 3.12 $\pm$ 0.63 & 2.75 $\pm$ 0.71 & 2.67 $\pm$ 0.72 \\
Framing & 4.23 $\pm$ 0.42 & 3.13 $\pm$ 0.36 & 3.04 $\pm$ 0.30 \\
Justification & 3.35 $\pm$ 0.48 & 2.46 $\pm$ 0.50 & 2.17 $\pm$ 0.38 \\
Knowledge integration & 4.32 $\pm$ 0.47 & 3.05 $\pm$ 0.28 & 2.94 $\pm$ 0.29 \\
Metacognition & 2.35 $\pm$ 0.55 & 1.24 $\pm$ 0.44 & 1.12 $\pm$ 0.35 \\
Uncertainty handling & 2.67 $\pm$ 0.66 & 1.57 $\pm$ 0.66 & 1.34 $\pm$ 0.49 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Evaluation scores across models of different capability levels. Higher-capacity models consistently receive higher scores across all criteria, indicating sensitivity of the evaluation framework to model quality differences.}
\label{table:model_comparison}
\end{table}

We observe a consistent ordering of models across all criteria: deepseek-v4-pro achieves the highest score, followed by gpt-4o-mini and phi-4-mini-instruct.
This ordering is preserved across all dimensions, indicating that the framework is sensitive to underlying model capability rather than producing unstructured or noise scores.
The largest performance gaps are observed in higher-order reasoning-related criteria, such as knowledge-integration and metacognition, suggesting that the framework is sensitive to differences in reasoning depth and reflective capability.
However, diversity exhibits smaller differences between models, indicating that this dimension is less strongly correlated with overall model capability.
These results provide evidence for the validity of the proposed evaluation framework.
