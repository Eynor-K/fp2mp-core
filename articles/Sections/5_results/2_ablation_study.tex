\subsection{\highlight{Ablation Study}}

To assess the contribution of the main architectural components of FP2MP, we conduct an ablation study by independently removing the blackboard, critic, ReDI mechanism, and external tool access.
These components were selected since they represent the key mechanisms responsible for agent coordination, reflection, routing, and external information acquisition, respectively. The results are reported in Table \ref{table:ablation_study}.

The ablation results indicate that the contribution of FP2MP components is dimension-dependent rather than uniform across the evaluation criteria.
Removing the blackboard produces the clearest reductions in coherence, decomposition, framing, and justification, suggesting that blackboard-based coordination primarily supports structured and coherent reasoning.
Removing ReDI leads to smaller but consistent reductions across several dimensions.

In contrast, removing the critic does not substantially reduce performance and even slightly improves several criteria, indicating a limited contribution under the current evaluation setting.
Similarly, removing external tool access improves diversity and knowledge integration, while producing only minor changes in the remaining dimensions.
Overall, the results suggest that the blackboard and ReDI primarily contribute to the organization of reasoning, whereas the critic and tool use do not uniformly translate into higher evaluation scores.
